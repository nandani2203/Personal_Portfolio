Numerical Investigation of Spray Characteristics of Lean-Burn Gas Turbine Injector

Proceedings of the 10th International and 50th National Conference on Fluid Mechanics and Fluid Power (FMFP) · December 2023 · IIT Jodhpur

Jet engines have to burn fuel "lean" to hit aggressive NOx emissions targets (regulators want cuts of up to 80%), but lean-burn combustion only works if the fuel injector atomizes fuel into a fine, evenly-distributed spray — and testing that with physical prototypes for every candidate swirler geometry is slow and expensive, often taking months per iteration.

We modelled a lean-burn pilot injector's co- and counter-rotating swirler assembly in ANSYS Fluent using a Discrete Particle Model, simulating droplet breakup under four air flow rates entirely in software before any hardware was built. The simulations show that increasing air flow rate both shrinks droplet size (finer atomization) and expands the central toroidal recirculation zone that anchors the flame, with the shear layer — not the core of the spray — dominating where droplets end up. Those relationships give injector designers a way to reason about geometry trade-offs before committing to a physical prototype.
